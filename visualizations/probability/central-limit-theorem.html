Central limit theorem

Average n random draws, repeat many times — the averages form a bell curve
Start with n = 1: the histogram just copies the source. Raise n: even a skewed or two-humped source gives a bell shape centred at μ, and the spread shrinks like σ/√n (n = 4 halves it, n = 16 quarters it).

About the concept

Take the average of n independent draws from almost any distribution with mean μ and finite standard deviation σ. As n grows, the distribution of that average becomes a bell curve centred at μ, whatever the original shape was, and its spread shrinks like σ/√n.

X̄n ≈ N(μ, σ²/n) for large n,    equivalently (X̄n − μ) / (σ/√n) → N(0, 1)

Why it matters: It explains why normal curves appear everywhere, and it justifies confidence intervals and hypothesis tests in statistics.
