About the concept

The slope of a secant line through two points of a curve is an average rate of change. As the second point slides towards the first (h → 0), the secant turns into the tangent line, and its slope is the derivative: the instantaneous rate of change at that point.

f′(x0) = limh→0 [f(x0 + h) − f(x0)] / h,   tangent: y = f(x0) + f′(x0)(x − x0)

Why it matters: Velocity, marginal cost and growth rates are all derivatives, and the tangent line is the best straight-line approximation of a function near a point.
