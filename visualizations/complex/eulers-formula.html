Euler's formula

e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ: walking θ units around the unit circle
As θ grows the point moves around the circle at speed 1; its shadows on the axes are cos θ and sin θ. At θ = π you reach −1, so e^(iπ) = −1. Make a negative to get e^((a+i)θ): a spiral — exactly the decaying oscillations of damped systems.

About the concept

The exponential with an imaginary exponent describes motion around the unit circle: eiθ is the point at angle θ, and its real and imaginary parts are cos θ and sin θ. The formula follows by comparing the Taylor series of ex, cos x and sin x.

eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ,    eiπ + 1 = 0,    z = r eiθ

Why it matters: It turns trigonometry into algebra with exponents, and it is the language of oscillations, signal processing, Fourier analysis and linear differential equations.
